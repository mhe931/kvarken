\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Abstract}\par

Earth Observation research requires more than image downloads: an infrastructure must validate
metadata, preserve the origin of observations, control external requests, support spatial
queries, and connect these operations to scientific applications. This thesis designs and
evaluates an in-house hybrid Earth Observation infrastructure for research and teaching within
Kvarken Space Center and the University of Vaasa. Hybrid here means standards-based external
data access combined with local persistence, processing, and an offline-safe engineering
baseline.

Platform suitability is assessed through documentary analysis rather than comparative user testing.

The work follows an engineering design-and-evaluation approach. Provider responses are
validated before transformation into a transport-neutral scene model. The prototype combines
bounded active concurrency, content-addressed payload storage, append-only provenance, an
embedded spatial catalog, metadata quality filters, and a read-only educational interface.
Students can use offline metadata fixtures, source validation, provenance inspection, spatial
queries, the read-only Application Programming Interface (API), and local real-data processing.
These are implemented exercises; learning outcomes were not evaluated.

The practical application examines sea-ice candidate mapping in eastern Kvarken using actual
Sentinel-1 radar observations and upstream CATALYST radiometric terrain-correction products
hosted by Microsoft Planetary Computer. Sentinel-2 observations provide conservative optical
proxy labels for snow-covered ice and dark water; European Space Agency (ESA) WorldCover 2021
v200 supplies an annual retrospective land mask, not a March 2021 ice observation. The
overlapping records define one case, not representative seasonal sampling. The optical scene
followed radar acquisition, so labels are not contemporaneous. A scene-specific linear
discriminant uses both radar polarisations with spatially separated training and evaluation
blocks.

Automated failure-oriented tests cover malformed payloads, timeouts and rate-limit responses;
a preserved synthetic benchmark evaluates engineering behavior under controlled conditions.
The scientific case study demonstrates the infrastructure's integration with a real Earth
Observation application, but does not establish operational ice-monitoring performance. Its
evaluation is limited to selected optical-proxy cells and retains uncertainty about upstream
noise treatment and illumination-angle effects. Because the labels represent snow-covered ice
and dark water rather than field observations, agreement is interpreted as a diagnostic
comparison within that selected domain. The model is scene-specific and has not been validated
across independent dates or locations.

Raster analysis ran locally. A five-scene metadata replay on cPouta, a cloud service of the
Finnish IT Center for Science (CSC), verified scene identities, persistent provenance, regional
queries, and the loopback API. This demonstrates a first-stage remote research and teaching
workflow, not image classification, distributed processing, improved classifier accuracy, or
cloud scalability. Further independent observations and educational evaluation remain future
work.
